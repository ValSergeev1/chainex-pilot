The dashboard

The day, in one place.

The dashboard is for the people who need to understand the operation without sitting on every route. It offers a calm view of the day: what needs attention, how people and partners are doing, and whether the work is on track. This page describes that view, and why it matters to drivers, shippers, and delivery partners.

An operating picture should be easy to read and hard to misunderstand. The dashboard is written in the language of the day — what needs a person, what is going well, and what can wait — rather than as a wall of figures that have to be decoded before anyone can act.

Starting with today

The first thing the dashboard shows is today. Not a generic homepage, and not a report that has to be assembled. A short summary of the operation, and then the exceptions: the items that need a decision or a follow-up before the rest of the picture will matter. A person who opens it for two minutes should leave knowing whether the day is healthy and, if it is not, where to look next.

From there, the view is arranged in a few clear sections — the work of the day, the people and partners in it, the money side of the operation, the obligations the business has to stay in front of, and the opportunities to grow. Each section can be opened for more detail without sending someone into a different product. The point is a single place to stand, not a set of doors to remember.

People, partners, and the work itself

Delivery is a people business. The dashboard keeps drivers, desks, and partners in the same picture as the routes they are running. If someone is not cleared to take work, if a partner is waiting on a decision, or if a team is carrying more than it should, that belongs in the open, next to the rest of today’s facts. It should not be a surprise discovered in a side conversation.

The same is true of the commitments around the work — safety, documentation, and the obligations a responsible operation has to keep current. The dashboard does not replace the people who own those responsibilities. It makes it harder for those responsibilities to sit out of sight while the rest of the day looks fine.

A view that holds up on a phone

People who run an operation are not always at a desk. The dashboard is laid out so the same sections, and the same idea — exceptions first, then the rest of the day — work on a phone. A manager who is in a yard, a partner who is checking in between meetings, or a coordinator who is already on the board can get the same honest picture without a different version of the truth.

Because the dashboard draws from the same work the driver app and the dispatch board are already using, it does not have to be reconciled at the end of the day. A ticket that was approved on the board, or a route that was closed by a driver, is part of the same picture when someone opens the dashboard a few minutes later.

Who this helps

Drivers

Drivers may not live in the dashboard, but they benefit when the people above the route can see the day clearly. A supervisor who can tell, quickly, which routes need support is in a better position to help before a problem becomes a missed window. Drivers should not have to be the only people carrying the full picture of a hard day.

Shippers

Shippers who have a program with ChainEx need a partner who can speak about the day with confidence: what moved, what did not, and what is being done about it. A dashboard that leads with exceptions, and that is tied to the work on the street, is how those conversations stay specific. It is also how a shipper can trust that the operating picture they are hearing is the same one the desk is using.

DSPs and carriers

Delivery service partners and carriers are often looking at the same questions from a slightly different angle: how their people are being used, whether they are meeting the commitments of the day, and where the next conversation with ChainEx should focus. A shared, readable dashboard means those conversations can start from the same facts — the work, the people, and the exceptions — instead of from two reports that do not quite agree.
